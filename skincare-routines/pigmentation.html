THE PROTOCOL — WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER

If the darkness has been there for years and looks different from your natural skin, it may be post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation or melasma rather than tan — actives still help, but get it checked by a dermatologist before months of self-treatment.

1 · EXFOLIATE

Glycolic / lactic acid (AHA) exfoliating toner, evenings, 2–3× per week — lifts the pigmented dead-cell layer. Start slow; never combine with physical scrubs the same day.

2 · TREAT

Targeted serum with kojic acid, alpha arbutin, tranexamic acid or vitamin C — tyrosinase inhibitors that slow new pigment while the old layer sheds.

3 · MOISTURIZE

Kojic / niacinamide / dark-spot cream on the area nightly (or an acid body lotion for body tan) — keeps the barrier intact so the actives don't irritate.

4 · PROTECT

SPF 30–50 broad-spectrum on the area every single morning. Non-negotiable: acids make skin sun-sensitive and skipping sunscreen undoes everything.

The honest timeline: skin turnover is ~28 days and pigment sits deep, so expect a minimum of 8–12 weeks (≈3 months) of consistent use for visible change — anyone promising 1-week removal is lying. Rules: patch-test every new product · start acids 2–3×/week, not daily · don't stack glycolic + kojic + scrubs on the same day · daily SPF on the area, reapplied outdoors · stop and see a dermatologist if you get burning, worsening darkness, or the patch is years old and looks unlike your natural skin (possible melasma / PIH).
